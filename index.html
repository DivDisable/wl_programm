Weightlifting Training Program

Day 1: Front Squat Focus

Today's Training Plan (Percentage-Based)

This program is based on modern weightlifting protocols, using **percentage-based training** to ensure consistent progress through progressive overload.

1. Warm-up

  • 5–10 minutes of light cardio (rower, treadmill, or cycling) to get the heart rate up.
  • Dynamic stretches for the legs and hips, such as leg swings, hip openers, and lunges.

2. Main Lift: Front Squat

The front squat is a key movement that builds strength and stability in the legs and core. In this session, we'll use a percentage of your **1RM (One-Rep Max)** for the sets.

Progressive Overload Protocol for Front Squats

  • Work up to a working set of 5 reps at **80%** of your 1RM. For example, if your 1RM is 100kg, you'll be lifting 80kg for your working sets.
  • Perform 4 sets of 5 reps at this weight. Rest 3–5 minutes between sets for full recovery.
  • Every 3–4 weeks, attempt to increase the working weight by 2.5–5kg (5–10lbs) based on your progress.

3. Secondary Lift: Deadlift (Volume-based)

Next, we'll move to the deadlift, focusing on volume and maintaining strength while keeping the load manageable.

  • 3 sets of 8 reps at **70%** of your 1RM.
  • Rest 3–4 minutes between sets. Focus on perfect form, especially with the lower back.

4. Accessory Lifts (Additional Leg Strength and Stability)

Now, we’ll focus on accessory lifts that help balance out the main lifts, targeting stability and muscle development.

  • Bulgarian Split Squats - 3 sets of 8–12 reps per leg (no percentage, use bodyweight or moderate weight)
  • Barbell Hip Thrusts - 3 sets of 8 reps at **60–70%** of your 1RM (use moderate weight)
  • Core Work (Planks or Russian Twists) - 3 sets of 30 seconds to 1 minute for planks, 15–20 reps per side for Russian twists

5. Cool Down

  • Stretch your quads, hamstrings, and hip flexors.
  • Use a foam roller to release tension in your quads and glutes.
  • Perform deep breathing exercises to help bring your heart rate down and relax your muscles.

Remember, each week you should aim to increase your lifting weight slightly (depending on progress and form). Be consistent and ensure you're getting adequate rest and nutrition to support your gains!
